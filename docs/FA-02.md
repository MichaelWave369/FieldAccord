# FA-02: deterministic WorkState and revocable attention advice

Status: candidate, review-only, offline. No tools, network activity, messages, or approvals.

## Problem

Work often spans model swaps, offline devices, interruptions and multiple collaborators. Loose transcripts make it too easy to silently promote an inferred claim to fact, a model's note to authority, or an attention preference to permission.

## Data contracts

- WorkEvent (v0.1) binds a WorkIntent digest to a single ordered WorkEvent stream.
- WorkState (v0.1) is *derived*, not independently authoritative. No snapshot in FA-02 may mint a grant.
- AttentionDecision (v0.1) is an advisory outcome: HOLD or REVIEW_CANDIDATE. No notification is dispatched.

Schemas are in contracts/v0.1/. The Python validator additionally checks canonical UUIDv4, exact field sets, calendar timestamps, hash continuity and legal state transitions.

## Deterministic event replay

Each event includes work_id, event_id, sequence, previous_hash, kind, actor claim, UTC timestamp, typed data, and event_hash = SHA256(canonical JSON of all other fields).

1. Sequence starts at 1 with WORK_OPENED and an exact digest of the given WorkIntent.
2. Every later event is contiguous, connected, same-work, unique by event ID and nondecreasing in declared timestamp.
3. Replay applies a frozen legal transition map.
4. A caller MAY supply expected_head to compare against a *separately trusted* anchored head.
5. A checkpoint records summary, pending items, and originating event ID. Checkpoints do not certify task completion.
6. Work may be OPEN, BLOCKED, NEEDS_REVIEW or CLOSED. CLOSED indicates withdrawn, superseded or archived, **never a claimed successful execution**.
7. Every WorkState has authority_granted=false, action_executed=false and notification_dispatched=false.

A corrupt entry, unexpected event, invalid transition, competing lease or missing chain link is refused rather than repaired silently. Replaying the same history produces an identical snapshot.

**Important:** A malicious source can recompute hashes for a rewritten chain. No signature, authenticated source, signed timestamp, append-only database, anchored consensus or durable storage is shipped here. A head is only useful as an anti-drift reference if a trusted component separately holds it.

## Attention leases

A human-*claimed* actor may set a lease for one recipient and one work item. This is a preference claim, **not identity verification**. A lease is active only in the advisory projection when:

- Its mode is no more intrusive than WorkIntent's original quiet / important / collaborate policy.
- Its expiry is after its declared set event, at most 24 hours later.
- It has not been explicitly revoked or invalidated by closing the work.
- The caller-supplied UTC evaluation time is at or after issuance and strictly before expiry.
- The recipient matches and the urgency passes the filter.

The same claimed human issuer must revoke a live lease before another is set. Events cannot be backdated within a chain without violating declared monotonicity, but timestamps themselves are untrusted.

Attention filter (advisory only):

| Lease | Routine | Important | Critical |
|---|---|---|---|
| quiet | HOLD | HOLD | REVIEW_CANDIDATE |
| important | HOLD | REVIEW_CANDIDATE | REVIEW_CANDIDATE |
| collaborate | REVIEW_CANDIDATE | REVIEW_CANDIDATE | REVIEW_CANDIDATE |

An absent, expired, revoked or recipient-mismatched lease always returns HOLD. No path returns SENT, APPROVED, AUTHORIZED or EXECUTED.

**Absolutely no communications are sent** by attention_review(). A later Commonline or other runtime MUST separately authenticate a human author, bind an actual attention grant, verify current recipient/consent, handle emergency safety policy independently, and explicitly authorize any real notification. This is deliberately not a safety alerting service.

## What FA-02 does not do

- It cannot authenticate the asserted human/agent actor or origin of a hash chain.
- It does not read an evidence source or evaluate claims inside free-text notes.
- It does not persist the event history to disk or sync across devices.
- It cannot deliver messages or take hardware, filesystem, browser, GitHub or tool actions.
- It does not grant execution authority, verify consent, or attest successful task completion.
- It does not implement transport replay defense across independently trusted devices.
